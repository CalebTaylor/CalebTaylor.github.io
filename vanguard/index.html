About the Team

van·guard

/ˈvanˌɡärd/ - noun
1: the forefront of an action or movement
2: the troops at the head of an army

The Vancouver Vanguard are an armored combat sports team, local to BC’s Lower Mainland, built around medieval martial traditions, modern athletic training, and a shared respect for the craft of fighting.

Founded by Josh Caldwell-Maki in 2019, the team has grown from a handful of core members to almost 20 active fighters, including a growing roster of female fighters.

Coming from various MMA and HEMA backgrounds, from veteran fighters to new talent, the Vancouver Vanguard is a tight-knitt team of armored combat enthusiasts.

We host weekly local practices, compete around the world, and perform exhibition fights at fairs and conventions across Canada.

The Vancouver Vanguard are closely affiliated with Canada's Historical Armored Combat Sports Association (HACSA)

What is Buhurt?

Historically, Buhurt (derived from the Old French word béhourd meaning to strike) was a form of medieval tournament and chivalric martial sport popular between the 13th and 17th centuries (1200-1600 AD) across Europe.

The present-day sport of Armored Combat, Armored MMA, or “Buhurt”, is a modern continuation of the tradition. Buhurt is a full-contact, full-speed, non-choreographed mixed martial arts discipline.

Using blunted weapons, modern safety guidelines, and historically accurate armor, fighters use a mix of swordsmanship, grappling, and striking to win points-based duels and team vs. team melees.

Glossary

Duels Using the same weapon loadout as their opponent (longswords, swords & shields, polearms, etc.), fighters aim to win the best of three 1-minute rounds of combat. The winner is determined by points earned from landing clean hits on their opponent, counted by ringside officials.
Profights Incorporating MMA (throws, kicks, punches, headbutts, takedowns) alongside fencing/swordfighting techniques, fighters aim to win the best of three 2-minute rounds, each fighter using the weapon loadout of their choice. A mix of points, aggression, ring control, and active pinning are considered when scoring matches.
Melees Takedowns, not strikes, are worth points in melees. Typically fought between two 5-person teams, using a mixture of different weapons and shields, fighters attempt to take down enemy fighters until one team achieves a ratio of 3:1 teammates still-standing, or better.

Melee matche sizes include 3 vs 3s, 5 vs 5s, 12 vs 12, 16 vs 16, and even 32 vs 32s.

Marshals

Marshals ensure the safety of both fighters and the crowd. They referee the match, enforce safety rules, and start/stop the match.
